# India ASOC exam — independent research for the india-hamexam edition

**Date:** 2026-10-09 (retrieval date for all sources below)
**Purpose:** Independent second look at the India Amateur Station Operator's Certificate (ASOC) examination, framed around what an `india-hamexam` edition of the us-hamexam study app would need. Cross-checks the October 2026 Stage 8A1 assessment rather than repeating it.
**Method:** Fresh web research. Read the verbatim Gazette text of the Telecommunications (Amateur Services) Rules, 2024 (via the NIAR-hosted mirror of the DOT source), rendered and read the scanned WPC FAQ letter of 05.12.2024 as images, and retrieved the 9 Nov 2024 WPC-attributed FAQ PDF directly.
**Access caveat:** Indian government sites (dot.gov.in, wpc.dot.gov.in, saralsanchar.gov.in) were unreachable/403; primary legal text was read via the NIAR mirror, which states its source as the DOT website. No question-bank content is reproduced — structure only.

## Engine implications (beyond data)

1. Mock-exam selection needs a **Part A / Part B blueprint** (25+25 / 50+50), not the US subelement/group scheme — a real engine change.
2. **Per-part scoring** (40% minima per part + 40% overall) is required *if* the Dec 2024 FAQ reflects practice — the current overall-only scorer must become part-aware.
3. Timer values need 3600s and **7200s** — already profile-driven via `examTimerSecondsValues`, so data change only.
4. Figures: not needed — set `figurePolicy` to none (already supported).
5. Morse cannot be an MCQ mock: a General mock must be written-exam-only with an explicit disclaimer; any Morse trainer is a separate practice feature, not exam-faithful.
6. No official question IDs exist → the author-curated bank needs its own ID scheme; US ID-syntax validators must not apply (already-flagged generalization).
7. Official section names are "Part A"/"Part B" — use those, not "Section A/B".
8. No bilingual need: exam is English-only.

## (b) Agreement / disagreement with the Stage 8A1 assessment

**Agreements (each independently re-verified):** 2024 Rules identity/dates; two categories; citizen/12-year eligibility; ₹100 exam fee; English-only; 40%-overall in the Rules text; the 05.12.2024 letter's per-part 40% requirement (scanned letter read directly — the conflict is real, and within that letter "Part A/Part B" unambiguously means the written paper's two parts); the 9 Nov 2024 FAQ's 40%-overall-only statement (retrieved directly — a plain unsigned 3-pager, so "not letterhead-verified" holds); 25+25/50+50 counts and 1h/2h durations; 8 wpm Morse tx+rx for General; no official public question pool found; Vigyan Prasar reuse terms unresolved; negative marking not found in any source.

**Corrections / additions:**
1. **Fee error:** the Gazette text reads "Two thousand rupees for lifetime validity" for ASOC (Restricted) — SCC Times' ₹20,000 is wrong by a zero. Any app copy must use ₹2,000.
2. **Minor provenance wrinkle:** the 05.12.2024 FAQ cites "GSR 657(E) dated 29.10.2024" — the actual notification is G.S.R. **675**(E). Likely a typo, but worth noting when citing it.
3. **New evidence:** exams are actively held under the new regime (2025 e-admit card, "Amateur Station Operating Certificate Examination – 2025", still carrying the Part A exemption field); the 11/03/2025 migration OM moved old licences to Saral Sanchar QR-code certificates; old-grade mapping confirmed (Grade II/Restricted → Restricted; Grade I/Advanced → General); "lifetime" is defined as till age 80.

## (c) Top 3 unresolved questions blocking an exam-faithful India mock

1. **Which pass rule is actually applied?** The Gazette says 40% overall; the letterheaded WPC FAQ (05.12.2024) adds 40% in each of Part A and Part B. This single question determines the scoring logic — it cannot be resolved from public sources and needs WPC clarification or observation of real result notices.
2. **No official question bank exists**, so any bank is author-curated practice content that must never be presented as an official or exact replica — a content blocker, not an engine one, and it caps how "exam-faithful" any mock can honestly claim to be.
3. **Syllabus status under Rule 5(3):** the CG was to publish the syllabus, but no new syllabus was found; the old syllabus appears to continue in use (2025 admit cards still reference Part A/B). Without a current official syllabus, the topic blueprint for question grouping rests on legacy documents.

**Secondary unknowns:** negative marking (no evidence either way — safest to assume none but verify); paper vs computer-based format; per-question marks (inference: equal marks → Restricted 2 marks/q, General 1 mark/q, from the 100-mark totals); whether the Part A degree-exemption should be surfaced in app copy (it appears on the 2025 admit card, so yes for Help text).

## Addendum (2026-10-09): pass-rule resolution

Per the user's domain knowledge of Indian administrative practice: once rules are gazetted, officials' clarifying letters govern practice by latest date, unless superseded by a still-later clarification. The 05.12.2024 letterheaded WPC FAQ (No. P-14036/02/2023-COP) is the latest dated clarification, so the applicable rule is **minimum 40% in Part A AND minimum 40% in Part B AND 40% overall**, for both ASOC categories. Blocker #1 above is therefore resolved as a decided scoring rule: the India edition's mock-exam scorer must be part-aware. Revisit only if a later WPC clarification appears.

## Addendum (2026-10-09): product positioning

With no official question pool and no officially specified question format, the user's decided positioning for india-hamexam is an honest **practical study tool**: authored questions and answers plus practical exam simulation (real structure — Part A/Part B split, 25+25 or 50+50, per-part 40% + overall 40% gates) so aspirants can prepare for the real exam. It must never be presented as official or as an exact replica of the examination. Blockers #2 and #3 are thereby downgraded from gates to content-authoring work: the question bank is curated practice mapped to the legacy syllabus topics, and the mock exam honestly mirrors the exam's *structure* while the questions themselves are practice.
