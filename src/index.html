New to Amateur Radio?


Curious what hams actually do beyond the exam — outdoors, on satellites, or just for fun?




New here?





Amateur ("ham") radio is a licensed hobby for two-way radio communication. The FCC issues three license classes — Technician, General, and Extra — earned by passing a written exam on rules, safety, and basic electronics (no Morse code required). This app studies the same official question pools used on that exam.

Getting started


    

  • One question is shown at a time; the correct answer is hidden during the selected recall period.
    • 

  • Tap Menu (☰) to open Settings, where you can change the question Pool, narrow it with Study scope (a subelement or group), set the Reveal after recall timer, change the Theme, and reach Mock Exam, Help & About, and Reset progress. The current pool name also shows next to Menu.
    • 

  • Study order: Sequential or Random (Mock Exam is unchanged).
    • 

  • Use Previous and Next at the bottom of the screen to move through questions.
    • 

  • Tap Reveal Now (also at the bottom) to show the correct answer immediately.
    • 

  • Set a recall timer with Reveal after to hide the answer for 5–60 seconds, or choose Never to keep it hidden. Your choice is remembered and restored the next time you open the app.
    • 

  • When a recall timer is running, a Pause / Resume button appears beside the countdown so you can stop and restart it. It is not shown when the timer is set to Never or once the answer is revealed.
    • 

  • Opening Settings pauses a running recall countdown at its exact remaining time; closing Settings back to the question resumes it from there, without losing time. This does not apply to the Mock Exam practice timer or to the figure viewer, which keep running while open.
    • 

  • Questions that refer to an official NCVEC diagram show that figure between the question and the answer choices, labelled with its figure number. The images are embedded in the file, so they work fully offline. Figures appear in study mode, in mock-exam questions, and in the results review.
    • 

  • Select Enlarge Figure <ID> to open the figure in a larger viewer. The viewer opens in Fit to window (the whole figure scaled to the available space); switch to Actual size to see it at its stored pixel size and scroll around it. Enlarging only changes how the stored image is displayed — it cannot recover detail that is not in the image. Close or Esc returns you to the button you opened it from, and any running timer keeps running the whole time.
    • 








Progress and bookmarks


    

  • Your position in All questions is saved automatically for each pool. A Study scope is temporary: it resets to All questions on reload, and browsing inside one never overwrites your saved full-pool position.
    • 

  • Bookmarks are saved per pool and identified by stable question IDs.
    • 

  • Reset progress clears saved question positions for all pools but does not remove bookmarks.
    • 

  • No account or sign-in is required.
    • 








Mock Exam


    

  • Tap Mock Exam to start a practice session for Technician, General, or Extra.
    • 

  • Setup opens with the pool you are currently studying already selected; choose a different pool from the dropdown if you want to practice another exam. Picking an exam pool does not change your study pool.
    • 

  • Each exam is built from the official NCVEC pool, one question per topic group.
    • 

  • Select an answer for each question and use Previous / Next to move around.
    • 

  • Questions that reference an official NCVEC diagram show that figure above the answer choices, and again beside the question in the results review. Enlarge Figure <ID> opens it in the larger viewer; the practice timer keeps running while the viewer is open.
    • 

  • Optional practice timer: keep Pool default (35 minutes for Technician and General, 50 for Extra), or choose a fixed 15, 30, 35, 50, or 60 minutes, or no timer. A fixed choice applies to every pool until changed; Pool default always follows the pool you are about to take. Your choice is remembered and restored the next time you open setup or the app, but does not affect an exam already in progress. The timer is an app practice aid and is not an FCC exam requirement.
    • 

  • Tap Finish Exam to submit. If any questions are unanswered, you will be asked to confirm submission.
    • 

  • If the practice timer reaches zero, the exam is submitted automatically.
    • 

  • The results view shows your score, the passing threshold, a subelement breakdown, and a review of every question.
    • 

  • Unanswered questions count as incorrect. This is an unofficial practice simulation, not an FCC examination.
    • 

  • Mock exam sessions and results are kept in memory only and are not saved to localStorage. Because of this, closing, reloading, or navigating away during an active exam will prompt your browser to confirm first — accepting the prompt discards the session.
    •

    Getting Started
    


    

    

    

    New to amateur ("ham") radio? This guide is a friendly starting point. Ham radio lets a licensed operator talk directly to other people by radio — across town, across the country, or off a satellite — using voice, text, or other digital modes, no internet or cell network required. The FCC exam this app helps you study for is just the entry ticket; what you do afterward is entirely up to you.
    


    Portable operation: a radio, an antenna, a notebook, and a view. No fixed station required.
    

    

    

    What people do with it
    

      

    • Parks on the Air (POTA): set up a portable station in a park for an afternoon and make contacts with other operators.
      • 

    • Hiking, camping, and mobile radio: carry a handheld radio on the trail, or install one in a vehicle to stay in touch on the road.
      • 

    • Satellites and the ISS: small satellites — and even astronauts aboard the International Space Station — relay amateur contacts with modest, affordable gear.
      • 

    • Digital modes and experimentation: send text, images, or data over radio, or build and modify your own equipment and antennas.
      • 

    • Emergency and public-service communication: trained operators support disaster response and community events when ordinary networks are down or overloaded.
      • 

    • Home stations, clubs, and mentors: build a station at home and join a local club, where experienced operators ("elmers") help newcomers learn by doing and make new friends along the way.
      • 

    

    

    

    A simple path
    

    Learn the basics here, practice with this app's Mock Exam, take the Technician exam at a local test session, then get on the air and pick whichever activity above interests you. There is no required order, and no obligation to try all of them.
